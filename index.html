Privacy Policy

Last updated October 5, 2026

The short version

  • Wormly keeps your library and your club so they're there on any phone you sign in on.
  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics companies, and your information is never sold.
  • People in your book club can see your name, your picture and the books on your shelf. Nobody else can.
  • If you use book talk, your messages go to our AI provider to write the replies. Your conversations are private to you.
  • You can delete your account, and everything tied to it, from inside the app.

Wormly is an app for keeping track of your books and reading with a book club. It's made by Gustavo Aguirre Jr, an independent developer ("Wormly", "we"). This policy explains what Wormly keeps, why, and what you can do about it. Wormly is currently in beta, so it may change as the app does; see Changes.

What Wormly keeps

Your account. You sign in with Apple. Apple gives Wormly an identifier for your Apple ID, your email address (or a private relay address, if you choose Hide My Email) and, the first time only, your name if you choose to share it.

Your profile. Your display name, your picture (one of Wormly's avatars, your initials, or a photo you choose), the genres and moods you like, and which way you started using the app.

Your library. The books you add and how you added them (scan, search or by hand), when you added them, which you're currently reading, which you've read, the tags you give them, the covers you choose, and photos you take of covers.

Your club. Which club you're in, when you joined, whether you run it, and the club's current pick.

Reports you send. If you report a problem with a book's details, the book and any note you write.

How the app is used. A simple record of each time you add a book (how you started, whether it worked, how long it took), which recommendations you open, and how you use book talk (for example, when you open it and how many messages you send, never what they say), plus daily counts of searches and book talk messages, so we can fix what's hard and keep costs in check. This stays in Wormly's own database; no analytics company is involved.

Wormly also keeps a copy of your library on your phone, so it works without a connection.

Camera and photos

Wormly asks for the camera to scan barcodes and photograph book covers, and for your photo library only when you choose a photo for your picture. When a book has no barcode, Wormly first tries to read its cover on your phone. If it can't, the cover photo is sent to our AI provider (see below) to read the title and author. That photo isn't stored, and it's sent without your name or account.

Book talk

On a book you're reading, have read, or your club is reading, you can talk about it with Wormly's AI. To write each reply, Wormly sends our AI provider (see below) your message and the conversation so far, the book's title, author and catalogue details, where you've said you are in the book, and the titles of a few unread books on your shelf, so it can suggest one to read next. It's sent without your name, email or account.

Wormly keeps your conversations and your place in each book, so you can pick up where you left off. They're private to you: not even your book club can see them. There's a daily limit on messages.

You can delete a conversation at any time from the ⋯ menu in book talk; it's removed from your phone and Wormly's servers. Wormly also keeps less over time: the extra working data stored alongside a conversation's replies is cleared after 30 days without use, and a conversation you haven't opened in 12 months is deleted.

Who can see what

Members of your book club can see your name, your picture, your favorite genres, the books on your shelf (including which you're currently reading and when you added them) and the club's pick.

Nobody else can see your read marks, your tags, your cover photos, your book talk conversations or your email address. Wormly has no public profiles and no messaging between people.

Who Wormly works with

Wormly relies on a few service providers to run. They get only what they need to do their part:

  • Supabase stores Wormly's database, sign-in and photos, on servers in the United States.
  • Apple handles Sign in with Apple, and is told when you delete your account so Wormly's link to your Apple ID is removed.
  • Anthropic (the AI provider) reads the title and author from a cover photo, and writes the replies in book talk, as described above. Under its commercial terms, Anthropic doesn't use what Wormly sends to train its models.
  • Hardcover and ISBNdb provide book details. Wormly's servers send them ISBNs, author names and what you search for, never who you are.
  • Cover images load directly from Open Library, ISBNdb and Hardcover, so those sites see your phone's internet address, as any website would.
  • Store links to Bookshop.org, Amazon and Audible only open when you tap them. Wormly may earn a commission on some purchases. Once you're on those sites, their own privacy policies apply.

Wormly doesn't sell your information, share it for advertising, or track you across other apps and websites.

How long it's kept

Your information is kept for as long as you have a Wormly account, except book talk conversations, which are deleted after 12 months without use (see Book talk). Signing out removes your library from that phone; it stays in your account for when you sign back in.

Deleting your account

Go to Profile → Delete account. This permanently deletes your account, profile, library, read marks, tags, covers, photos, book talk conversations and club membership, from your phone and from Wormly's servers, and removes Wormly from your Apple ID's Sign in with Apple list. If you ran a club, it passes to the member who's been there longest.

A few things aren't tied to you and remain: reports you sent about book details (without your name), and Wormly's shared catalogue of book information, including anonymous search results. Copies may also remain in our provider's backups for a short time before they expire.

Children

Wormly isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has an account, contact us and we'll delete it.

Your choices

You can change your name, picture and genres in the app, and delete your account at any time. To ask what Wormly holds about you, or for anything else about your privacy, email us.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date. If a change affects how your information is shared, we'll tell you in the app first.
